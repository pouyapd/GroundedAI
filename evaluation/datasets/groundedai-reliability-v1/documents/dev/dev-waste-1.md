Thornmere District Council – Household Waste and Recycling Collection Policy

Valid from 1 March 2026. This policy explains how Thornmere District Council collects household waste and recycling from the 41,300 homes in the district. It replaces the collection policy of 2023 and applies to every household that pays council tax to Thornmere, including flats above shops. The service is run by the Environmental Services team, which is led by the Head of Environmental Services, Ruth Abernathy-Coles.

Each house receives four containers. The grey bin (180 litres) is for general waste and is emptied every two weeks. The blue bin (240 litres) is for mixed dry recycling such as paper, card, food tins, drinks cans and plastic bottles, and is emptied every two weeks on the alternate week to the grey bin. The brown caddy (23 litres) is for food waste and is emptied every week. The green bin (240 litres) is for garden waste and is only provided to households that subscribe to the garden waste service. Blocks of flats with eight or more homes share larger communal bins of 1,100 litres instead, which are emptied on the same schedule.

Containers must be at the kerbside by 06:30 on the collection day and should be taken back in by 20:00 the same day. Crews work between 06:30 and 16:00, so a bin may be emptied at any time in that window. Lids must be fully closed. Crews will not empty a bin that weighs more than 60 kilograms, and they will not take extra bags placed on top of or beside a bin, because of the risk of injury and spillage. The only exceptions are household batteries and small electrical items set out as described in the Waste Services FAQ, and extra recycling during the Christmas period.

Households of six or more people can apply for a larger grey bin of 240 litres. The application form is WR-14 and must include proof of the number of residents, such as a council tax letter. Decisions on larger bins are made within 10 working days. Households that produce medical waste at home, for example from home dialysis, can receive an extra weekly collection free of charge. This collection is arranged through the Clinical Waste Officer, Daniel Ferreira-Hughes.

The garden waste service costs £52 per bin for the 2026–27 season, which runs from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027. Residents who hold a Thornmere Council Tax Reduction award pay a reduced price of £26 per bin. A household may subscribe for up to three green bins. Garden waste is collected every two weeks from March to November, and once a month in December, January and February. The price is the same whenever in the season a subscription is bought, so subscriptions bought after 31 May are not reduced.

Bulky items such as sofas, beds, wardrobes and fridges are collected for a fee. The standard charge is £35.00 for up to three items and £8.50 for each further item, with a maximum of eight items per booking. Residents who receive Council Tax Reduction pay half of the standard charge. Bookings must be made at least five working days before the collection date. Each booking has a reference number starting with the letters BW, and this reference must be written on a label attached to every item.

Bulky items must be left outside by 07:00 on the collection day, in a place the crew can reach without entering a building. Mattresses must be wrapped in plastic. The Council does not collect building rubble, car parts, tyres, gas bottles or asbestos as bulky waste.

If a bin has not been emptied, residents should report it within two working days of the scheduled collection day. Reports made after that are not accepted, and the bin will be emptied on the next normal collection day. The Council aims to return for a reported missed bin within three working days. If the crew recorded that the bin was not out by 06:30, was contaminated or was overweight, the bin will not be collected again until the next scheduled day.

Recycling that contains the wrong items is treated as contaminated. The crew leaves a yellow tag on a contaminated blue bin and does not empty it. After a third yellow tag in any 12-month period, an Education Officer visits the household to explain the rules. Repeated contamination after that visit can lead to a fixed penalty notice of £80, which is reduced to £50 if it is paid within 14 days.

Residents who cannot move their bins because of age, disability or illness, and who have no one else in the home aged over 16 who can help, can apply for an assisted collection. With an assisted collection, the crew collects the bins from an agreed point on the property and returns them there afterwards. Assisted collections are reviewed every two years.

New and replacement bins are delivered within 15 working days of the request. A replacement grey or blue bin costs £28, except where a Council crew damaged the bin, in which case it is replaced free of charge. Bins for newly built homes are paid for by the developer at £96 per property for a full set. Bins remain the property of the Council and must stay at the address when a resident moves house.

The Council runs one Household Recycling Centre, at Sandpiper Road. It is open 08:00–18:00 from April to September and 08:00–16:00 from October to March, and it is closed on 25 and 26 December and on 1 January. Vans, and trailers longer than 2.5 metres, need a permit, and each household can receive up to 12 van permits per year. Residents must show proof of address on entry.

Questions about this policy can be sent to the Waste Services Contact Centre on 01632 960 418, which is open Monday to Friday, 08:30–17:00. Formal complaints are answered within 20 working days.
